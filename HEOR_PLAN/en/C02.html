How many services does a community need? Need, demand, utilisation and community health needs assessment

Key ideas
  • Need, want, demand and utilisation are treated in these notes as four different quantities (the textbook describes demand as the combined result of the other components1); utilisation is the only one measured directly, and it is filtered by the existing supply.
  • Recommended standards (services per 100,000 population) give a normative benchmark, not a measure of the need of a particular population23.
  • Future demand is projected by multiplying current age-specific rates by the projected population in the same age groups; the assumptions of the projection are written down explicitly4.
  • Community health needs assessment goes through the environmental assessment, baseline data, the community profile, synthesis, prioritisation and the implementation plan56.
  • Quantitative methods tell how much; qualitative methods tell why; a planning question usually needs both7.
  • When data on need are missing, a plan starts from hospital utilisation and writes down its assumption; utilisation shows how much of the need has been met, not how large the need is8.
  • A figure from an official document is checked against another series before it is used: a row labelled “incidence” may count something other than new cases9.

Module objectives

§By the end of the module, you will be able to:

  • distinguish the need for health services, want, demand and utilisation, explain why the four never coincide and use recommended standards without confusing them with need;
  • list and explain the factors that influence demand (demographic, socioeconomic, technological and structural), including the price elasticity of demand;
  • measure the utilisation of a service through the established indicators (admissions, inpatient days, average length of stay, bed occupancy rate, consultations) and project demand as the product of a utilisation rate and the population projection;
  • go step by step through a community health needs assessment, from the environmental assessment and baseline data to synthesis, prioritisation and the implementation plan;
  • choose the research method suited to a planning question (quantitative or qualitative: the survey, the focus group, the key informant interview, observation) and justify the choice;
  • read an indicator from an official planning document critically and check the figure against another series before using it;
  • project deaths from a disease by age group and say what a plan would decide on the basis of the projection and what assumptions it hides;
  • find, in Romanian and European data sources, the data needed for a needs assessment and read them with their limitations.

Preparing for the module

§The module takes place online, in Microsoft Teams, and lasts four hours. Before the module, check your camera, microphone and connection in a Teams test meeting; if you cannot use the camera, tell the course leader in the chat when you join. Mentimeter is used for opinion questions and for the teams' sentences, and its code appears in the meeting chat; at the end there is a Kahoot on the concepts of the session. For the calculation annex you need a calculator and a spreadsheet (Excel or an equivalent program).

§As preparation, think of a health service you know well (a ward, an outpatient clinic, a programme of the health insurance house, a product of the company you work for). Write down in one sentence: how many people in the population served would need it in a year, and where you would get the figure from. You will compare your answer with the methods in the module.

§The module starts from a real document: the regional master plan for health services for the West region, published by the Ministry of Health in 202310. You work in teams of three or four people, in separate Teams rooms (breakout rooms), on a calculation annex in two parts: the first about palliative care in Hunedoara county, the second about stroke in the region. A few teams present their annex at the end.

§The outline, in brief:

  • part one: what the plan could not calculate, as told by the course leader, who coordinated its preparation;
  • part two: palliative care in Hunedoara; what the plan proposes, then need, demand, utilisation, standards and projection, applied to the county's data, and the first part of the calculation annex;
  • part three: stroke in the West region; the patient pathway, a figure that cannot be used as incidence, the data that can be used and the second part of the annex;
  • part four: community health needs assessment, research methods and data sources, linked to the first practical activity (PA1, the community profile);
  • part five: presentation of the annexes, the course leader's commentary and the final Kahoot.

§There are three new terms we use most often: the need for services (what a population should receive, according to professional judgement), demand (what people actually ask for) and community health needs assessment (CHNA), the process through which a community measures its needs and ranks them. The rest of the vocabulary is explained in these notes.

The module case

§The case is real. In 2023 the Ministry of Health published the regional master plan for health services for the West region, which covers Arad, Caraș-Severin, Hunedoara and Timiș counties, prepared in a project co-financed by the European Social Fund10. The course leader coordinated the preparation of this master plan. The part on palliative care focuses on Hunedoara, a county with an ageing and declining population in which the number of deaths is set to rise, according to official projections. Among Romania's counties, excluding Bucharest and Ilfov, Hunedoara has the fifth highest share of people aged 65 and over (23.8%), after Teleorman (26.6%), Caraș-Severin (24.4%), Vâlcea (24.3%) and Brăila (24.1%), and the number of deaths is set to rise by 23.4% between 2024 and 204011. Because this case contains the documented answers for Hunedoara, the county is not assigned to teams for the practical activities and is not an examination county.

§On 1 January 2025, Hunedoara county had 351,253 inhabitants12. Of these, 83,666 were aged 65 and over, that is 23.8% of the population13, and 18,047 were aged 80 and over14. In 2024, 5,440 residents of the county died15. The question of the module is the one a regional plan asks itself: how many palliative care services does the county need, and how do we know?

Applied activity 5 min

§Before any figure, each participant answers an opinion question in Mentimeter: if you had had to write the palliative care chapter of the plan yourself, which piece of data would you have missed most? The answers are read on the spot; at the end of part one, the course leader's account shows which data were actually missing.
